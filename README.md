# Netflix India: Micro-Subscription & Acquisition Strategy

## Project Overview
This repository contains a detailed product strategy and business case proposing the launch of 2-day and 7-day micro-subscription plans for Netflix India[cite: 45, 51]. Developed by Meet Badjatya, the project aims to capture demand during high-intent periods—such as weekend viewing, new season premieres, and holidays—targeting India's price-sensitive digital audience[cite: 45, 51].

## The Business Problem
Despite India's OTT audience reaching 601 million users by Q2 2025, Netflix India's paid subscriber base is only slightly above 12.3 million (Q3 2025), representing a penetration rate of roughly 2%[cite: 47, 51]. 

Key market friction points include:
* **Affordability & Piracy:** The cost of Subscription Video-on-Demand (SVOD) is a major barrier for price-sensitive consumers, pushing them toward shared accounts, torrents, or free pirated sites[cite: 49].
* **Aggressive Ecosystem Competition:** JioHotstar dominates with a 25% SVOD market share by bundling live sports and telecom plans, while Amazon Prime Video (23% share) leverages its massive e-commerce delivery ecosystem[cite: 48, 49]. 
* **Inflexible Pricing:** Netflix's monthly commitment model limits its reach among users who only want to watch specific trending shows or binge during weekends[cite: 51].

## Targeted User Personas
* **Mihir (19, College Student, Mumbai):** Highly engaged with global pop culture and social media trends, but relies on pocket money and shared accounts. Experiences FOMO when missing trending shows[cite: 48].
* **Ritika M (30, IT Consultant, Bangalore/Delhi):** A busy professional with unpredictable hours who only watches Netflix on weekends or vacations. She dislikes paying for a full month when her viewing time is highly limited[cite: 48].

## Strategic Solution: Micro-Subscriptions
To capture these untapped segments, the strategy introduces short-term micro-subscription passes designed for immediate, low-barrier access[cite: 51]:
* **Mobile Tier:** ₹35 (2-Day) and ₹69 (7-Day)[cite: 51].
* **Basic Tier:** ₹49 (2-Day) and ₹89 (7-Day)[cite: 51].
* **Standard Tier:** ₹199 (7-Day)[cite: 51].

*Pricing Psychology:* The 7-day plans are priced at 35-50% of the standard monthly model, while the 2-day plans sit at roughly 25%[cite: 51]. This structure ensures a higher per-day revenue yield and naturally nudges frequent buyers toward full monthly subscriptions for better value[cite: 51].

## Prioritization & Rollout (S.M.A.R.T. Framework)
* **Specific:** Launch micro-subscription plans across Netflix India tiers to acquire 2.5M users in 6 months, converting 15% to monthly plans within 12 months[cite: 52].
* **Measurable:** Generate 175 Cr ($21M) in revenue by month 6 and achieve a 35% repeat purchase rate[cite: 52].
* **Achievable & Relevant:** Aligns with Indian digital payment behaviors (micro-transactions) and offers an ad-free premium experience for budget-conscious viewers[cite: 52].
* **Time-Bound Execution:**
  * *Phase 1 (Months 0-3):* Pilot across 5 metros targeting 500K users[cite: 52].
  * *Phase 2 (Months 4-6):* Nationwide rollout targeting 2.5M users[cite: 52].
  * *Phase 3 (Months 7-12):* Focus on retention and the 15% conversion goal to monthly subscriptions[cite: 52].

## Metrics Framework
* **North Star Metric:** Monthly Active User Retention Rate and Net Revenue Impact:
  $$\text{Net Revenue Impact} = [(\text{New Micro-user Revenue} + \text{Converted Monthly Revenue}) - \text{Cannibalized Monthly Revenue}]$$
[cite: 53]
* **Supporting Metrics:**
  * *Acquisition:* Micro-subscription sign-ups, Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), and tier 2-3 geographic penetration[cite: 54].
  * *Engagement:* Average watch time per session and content completion rate (>60%)[cite: 54].
  * *Monetization:* Revenue Cannibalization Rate (must stay <5% to prevent net revenue decline) and Repeat Purchase Rate[cite: 54].

## Potential Risks & Mitigation
* **Severe ARPU Cannibalization:** Existing monthly subscribers downgrading to micro-plans, creating a perpetual low-value cohort[cite: 56]. 
* **Brand Premiumization Damage:** Micro-subscriptions could position Netflix as a "cheap" option, eroding its luxury brand perception and reducing long-term pricing power[cite: 56].
* **Data Dilution:** Sporadic viewing from micro-users generating fragmented data, which could degrade the accuracy of Netflix's core recommendation engine[cite: 56].
